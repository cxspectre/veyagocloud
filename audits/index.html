Your software bill went up this year. Did your use of it?

Why it pays for itself

Less than one year of the waste it finds.

Take a 60-person team on Microsoft 365 E3 at $39 per user per month. Finding the 15% that goes unused or oversized, which is typical, saves about $4,200 a year, every year. The audit costs $2,490, once.

Security findings don't show up on an invoice, but a forwarding rule nobody noticed or an admin account without MFA is where many small-business breaches start.

Cassian Drefke

Leads every audit. He has led two large Microsoft 365 tenant consolidations end to end, including cross-tenant mail, calendar and SharePoint migrations.

What you get

A report you can act on the next day.

Every finding has the same six fields: a number, what we found, why it matters, how serious it is, what it saves a year, and the exact setting to change and where.

  1. A one-page summary for whoever signs the budget: yearly savings, the five fixes that matter most, and an overall risk rating
  2. License savings, line by line: what you pay now and what you would pay
  3. Security findings, ranked by risk
  4. Housekeeping: stale sites, guests, storage
  5. A fix plan for the next week, month and quarter
  6. The raw data as CSV, for your IT

What we check

Twenty-four checks, each a line in the report.

This is the whole list for the Audit, with where each answer comes from. The Deep Audit adds the last three.

Licenses

  • 1.1Seats purchased, assigned and left unassigned, for every plan Graph: subscribedSkus
  • 1.2Licensed users with no sign-in for 30, 60 and 90 days Entra sign-in activity
  • 1.3Right-sizing: people on E3, E5 or a Premium plan who only use the basics, such as no desktop apps, Intune or Defender Microsoft 365 usage reports
  • 1.4Shared, room and resource mailboxes holding a paid license they don't need Exchange Online
  • 1.5Add-ons bought twice and plans that overlap, such as a standalone Teams Phone on top of a bundle that already includes it Graph: licenseDetails
  • 1.6Renewal dates, monthly or annual commitment, and how exposed you are to the next price rise Admin center billing

Accounts

  • 2.1Leavers' accounts: disabled but still licensed, or enabled but unused Entra ID
  • 2.2Guest users: how many, when they were last active and who invited them Entra ID
  • 2.3Service and shared accounts that can still sign in interactively Entra ID

Identity

  • 3.1MFA coverage per person, and legacy sign-in methods still allowed Entra authentication methods
  • 3.2Admin roles: how many Global Admins, admins without MFA, standing against eligible roles Entra roles
  • 3.3Break-glass accounts: present, and excluded from the right policies Conditional Access
  • 3.4Conditional Access policies: gaps, policies left in report-only, exclusions Conditional Access

Mail

  • 4.1SPF, DKIM and DMARC for every domain, and how strict the DMARC policy is DNS and Exchange Online
  • 4.2Mail forwarded to outside addresses, and suspicious inbox rules Exchange Online
  • 4.3Anti-phishing and impersonation protection settings Defender for Office 365

Files and sites

  • 5.1External sharing for every SharePoint site and OneDrive, and "anyone" links in use SharePoint admin
  • 5.2Stale Teams and sites: no activity in 180 days, no owner, or a single owner Graph and usage reports
  • 5.3Storage used against your quota, and what extra storage costs you SharePoint admin
  • 5.4Copilot readiness: sensitive sites everyone can read, and overshared files SharePoint permissions

Apps, devices and logging

  • 6.1Third-party apps with consent, high-risk permissions, and who is allowed to consent Entra enterprise apps
  • 6.2App registrations with secrets that have expired or are about to Entra app registrations
  • 7.1Intune enrollment, compliance policies, and unmanaged devices reaching company data Intune
  • 8.1Whether the unified audit log is on, and how long it keeps records Purview

Straight answers

Before you ask.

Do you need admin access?

No. We use Microsoft's read-only roles. We see settings and usage, and can't change anything.

What happens to our data?

We collect only what the audit needs, keep it for the engagement, and delete it within 30 days of delivering the report. A data processing agreement is available before we start.

Do you sell Microsoft licenses?

No. We earn nothing from what you buy or cancel.

What if you don't find savings?

Then you have written proof that your tenant is in good shape, which your auditor, board or insurer will want anyway.

How is this different from Secure Score?

Secure Score tells you what Microsoft thinks is missing. It doesn't tell you what you're overpaying for, which accounts belong to people who left, or which fix matters most for your business.

Who does the work?

Cassian Drefke leads every Microsoft 365 and software audit personally, and he is the person you talk to from the scope call to the walkthrough.

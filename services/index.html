Applied intelligence

How we work

One team across the entire engagement - no account manager between you and the builders, no handoff between design and engineering. Small, deliberate, and focused on doing each project properly.

The process

From sketch to ship.

  1. 1

    Discover

    Work out what the product actually needs to do - not what was specified, but what users genuinely need. We cut scope early and deliberately.

  2. 2

    Design

    Architecture, flows, visual design, and design tokens - with engineering constraints in front of us from the start. No handoff. No redesign after build begins.

  3. 3

    Build

    Flutter for cross-platform, native Swift where the platform demands it, Supabase for backend, on-device ML where privacy requires it. Built to last.

  4. 4

    Ship

    Store listing, ASO, screenshots, and the pre-submission polish that separates an app that gets featured from one that gets lost.

For clarity

What we don't build.

We don't take on projects designed to harvest user data for advertising, build engagement mechanics that exploit vulnerabilities, or ship dark patterns. We don't sign NDAs that require us to pretend we built something we didn't. And we don't take on more than we can do well - if the fit isn't right, we'll say so at the start.

Before you write

How an engagement actually works.

How much does a product engagement cost?

Product work is quoted per project rather than from a price list, because the scope is different every time. You get one number in writing, with what is included and what is not, before anything is charged - the same rule as our fixed-price website work, which starts at $699 and is published in full on the Websites page.

How long does a project take?

The Discover stage sets the window, and it goes in the written quote before you commit. For comparison, our fixed-price website work runs from 7 to 10 working days for a single page to four to eight weeks for a site with a dashboard behind it. Product engagements are larger than that and are scoped individually.

Is this a retainer or a project?

A project. We take on a small number of engagements a year with a defined scope and an agreed end, rather than selling hours by the month. If a product needs ongoing attention after launch we agree that separately.

Who owns the code and the App Store listing?

You do. On final payment the code, the repository and the listing are yours, and you can take them to any other team. That is written into our terms rather than negotiated case by case.

How do payments work?

Against an invoice from Veyago Inc., never before you have accepted a written quote. Website work is half to schedule and half at launch; larger product engagements are split into milestones set out in the quote.

What kind of work do you turn down?

Anything designed to harvest user data for advertising, engagement mechanics that exploit vulnerabilities, and dark patterns. We also decline work we cannot do well, and we say so at the start rather than after a deposit.

Can you take over an app someone else built?

Sometimes. It depends on what it is built in and how it is put together, which is the first thing Discover looks at. If taking it over is the wrong call we will tell you that instead of quoting for it.
